17 Best Free Things to Do in Amsterdam

After my fifth visit to the Dutch capital this spring, I realized how much the city offers for zero Euros. Navigating Amsterdam on a budget is surprisingly easy if you know where the locals go to relax and explore. Our editors have vetted every spot on this list to ensure they remain accessible and high-quality for travelers. This guide was last refreshed in June 2025 to reflect the latest ferry schedules and garden opening hours.

Saving money in Amsterdam allows you to splurge on a nice dinner or a historic canal cruise later. You do not need a high-priced ticket to experience the city's unique industrial history or its serene Renaissance gardens. Whether you are a solo backpacker or a family, these activities provide a deep look into local culture. Check our Amsterdam on a Budget: 10 Essential Money-Saving Tips guide for more ways to save during your stay.

17 Best Free Things to Do in Amsterdam

I have grouped these 17 activities into four clusters: iconic views, art and music, local neighborhoods, and outdoor recreation. This structure helps you plan your day based on your current location or specific interests. Iconic views and transport options are perfect for your first morning in the city center. Art and music gems provide cultural depth without the high museum price tags found elsewhere.

Many of these attractions are located within walking distance of the central canal ring. Others require a short, scenic trip across the water to the trendy north side of the city. Wander through the Jordaan district to find many of the hidden courtyards mentioned below. Remember that while entry is free, some spots may have limited hours on Sundays or public holidays.

If you have more time, follow our 3-day Amsterdam itinerary to see how these fit into a full trip. Most of these locations are easily accessible via the city's extensive tram and bus network. Always keep a digital map handy to find the smaller entrances to gardens and galleries. The following list represents the very best zero-cost experiences available in the city today.

  1. Take a Free Scenic Ferry Ride to Amsterdam-Noord
    • These blue-and-white GVB ferries depart from behind Central Station to cross the IJ River toward the industrial NDSM Wharf.
    • It costs nothing for pedestrians and offers a ten-minute mini-cruise with fantastic skyline views.
    • Boats run 24/7, though the NDSM route runs less frequently after midnight.
    • Stand at the very front for the best photos of the passing ships.
  2. Explore the Rijksmuseum Gardens and Sculptures
    • While the museum interior requires a ticket, the surrounding Renaissance-style gardens are completely free to the public.
    • You can admire rotating sculpture exhibits by world-famous artists among manicured hedges and historic fountains.
    • The gardens typically open daily from 9am to 6pm, offering a peaceful escape in Museumplein.
    • Look for the giant finger sculpture near the entrance for a quirky photo opportunity.
  3. Visit the OBA Central Library at Oosterdok
    • This modern architectural marvel houses over 1.5 million items and offers some of the best views in the city.
    • Entry is free for everyone, and the top floor features a public terrace with panoramic vistas of the harbor.
    • The library is open daily from 10am to 10pm, making it a great rainy-day refuge.
    • Head straight to the seventh floor to find the outdoor balcony without paying for the cafe.
  4. Relax in Vondelpark and the Open Air Theater
    • As the city's most famous green space, Vondelpark is perfect for people-watching and afternoon picnics.
    • During the summer months from June to August, the Open Air Theater hosts free concerts and dance performances.
    • The park is open 24/7 and is easily reached via Trams 1, 2, or 5.
    • Check the theater schedule online to catch the popular Friday night jazz sessions.
  5. Admire Street Art and Graffiti at NDSM Wharf
    • This former shipyard has transformed into a vibrant cultural hub filled with massive murals and industrial art.
    • You can wander the outdoor grounds for free to see works by world-renowned street artists like Kobra.
    • The area is accessible via the free ferry from the back of Central Station.
    • Visit on a weekday morning to explore the shipyard cranes without the weekend crowds.
  6. Wander Through the Jordaan and Hidden Hofjes
    • The Jordaan is famous for its narrow streets and 'hofjes,' which are historic hidden courtyards built for the elderly.
    • While most are private, several like the Karthuizerhof are open to respectful visitors during daylight hours.
    • These quiet oases offer a glimpse into 17th-century life away from the busy canals.
    • Look for the small unlabelled wooden doors that often lead to these secret gardens.
  7. Join Free Festivals Like the Amsterdam Light Festival
    • The city hosts several major events where the primary attractions cost nothing to view from the street.
    • The Amsterdam Light Festival features stunning installations along the canals every winter from December to January.
    • King's Day in April turns the entire city into a massive, free orange-themed street party.
    • Walk the designated festival routes to see the best art without paying for a boat tour.
  8. Sample Dutch Cheese at Henri Willig Shops
    • While these are retail shops, they offer a 'budget-hacker' experience with extensive free cheese tasting stations.
    • You can try dozens of varieties of Gouda and Edam while learning about the traditional Dutch cheesemaking process.
    • Shops are located throughout the city center and are usually open from 9am to 9pm.
    • Try the smoked goat cheese or the pesto-infused varieties for a unique local flavor.
  9. Take a Dive at the Marineterrein Swimming Spot
    • Located near the National Maritime Museum, this former naval base is now a popular public swimming area.
    • It is completely free to use and features clean water, wooden decks, and a relaxed summer vibe.
    • The area is open 24/7, though swimming is best during daylight for safety reasons.
    • Watch out for the occasional boat traffic and check the local water quality signs before jumping in.
  10. Play the Public Piano at Amsterdam Central
    • Inside the main hall of the station, a public piano invites commuters and travelers to share their music.
    • You can often hear talented locals playing everything from classical pieces to modern pop during the morning rush.
    • The piano is available whenever the station is open, typically from early morning until midnight.
    • Sit on the nearby benches for a few minutes to enjoy a free, impromptu concert.
  11. Enjoy 360° City Views From the NEMO Rooftop
    • The massive copper-green roof of the NEMO Science Museum serves as the city's largest public rooftop square.
    • Access is free via the stairs on the side of the building, even without a museum ticket.
    • The terrace is open daily from 10am to 5:30pm and features a water fountain for kids.
    • Bring your own snacks to enjoy a budget lunch with one of the best views in town.
  12. Experience the Civic Guards Gallery (Schuttersgalerij)
    • This glass-roofed passage is a free 'museum street' located between Kalverstraat and the Amsterdam Museum.
    • It displays massive 17th-century portraits of the city's historic guards alongside modern Dutch art.
    • The gallery is open daily from 10am to 5pm and requires no ticket to enter.
    • Look for the giant wooden statue of Goliath that stands guard at one end of the hall.
  13. Explore the Red Light District by Day
    • Walking through De Wallen during the day reveals the historic architecture and the Oude Kerk without the nighttime chaos.
    • You can see the 'Belle' statue and the Prostitution Information Center to learn about the area's complex history.
    • The district is a public neighborhood and is free to walk through at any hour.
    • Remember that taking photos of the windows is strictly forbidden and can lead to trouble.
  14. Go For Live Music at Lotti’s Sunday Live
    • Located inside The Hoxton hotel, this weekly event features live bands and a trendy local atmosphere.
    • Entry is free for everyone, providing a 'gezellig' vibe perfect for a lazy Sunday afternoon.
    • Music typically starts around 4pm and lasts into the early evening.
    • Arrive early to snag one of the comfortable velvet sofas near the stage.
  15. Stroll the Canal Ring to the Seven Bridges View
    • The intersection of Reguliersgracht and Keizersgracht offers a unique view where you can see seven arched bridges in a row.
    • This iconic Amsterdam landmark is best seen at night when the bridges are beautifully illuminated.
    • The area is a public thoroughfare and costs nothing to explore on foot or by bike.
    • Stand on the corner of Thorbeckeplein to get the perfect straight-line shot of all seven arches.
  16. Attend a Free Concert at Bimhuis or Concertgebouw
    • Bimhuis often hosts 'Monday Match' sessions where musicians and dancers perform experimental sets for free.
    • Additionally, the world-famous Concertgebouw offers free lunchtime concerts on select Wednesdays at 12:30pm.
    • Check the Concertgebouw Free Lunchtime Concerts Schedule for current dates.
    • Arrive at least 45 minutes early as these popular sessions frequently reach full capacity.
  17. Browse the Albert Cuyp Market
    • As the largest outdoor market in Europe, the Albert Cuyp is a sensory feast of sights, smells, and sounds.
    • Walking through the stalls is free and offers a deep dive into the multicultural heart of the De Pijp neighborhood.
    • The market runs Monday through Saturday from 9am to 5pm.
    • Keep an eye out for the stroopwafel stands where you can often watch the cookies being made.

Essential Tips for Navigating Amsterdam on a Budget

Walking is the most effective way to see the city center without spending a dime. Most major sights are located within a three-mile radius of Dam Square. If you need to cover longer distances, consider a multi-day GVB pass for the best value. Check the GVB Ferry Timetables for late-night service to the north side.

Public restrooms are notoriously expensive and hard to find in the city center. The OBA Central Library and large department stores like De Bijenkorf offer the most reliable facilities. Always carry a reusable water bottle to fill up at the many public 'Kraanwater' taps found in squares. Drinking tap water is safe and will save you several Euros per day compared to bottled water.

Supermarkets like Albert Heijn are your best friend for affordable meals on the go. You can find pre-made salads and sandwiches for under five Euros, which is much cheaper than canal-side cafes. Picnicking in Vondelpark or Museumplein is a common local tradition during the warmer months. Many locals also use the 'FEBO' snack walls for a quick, hot Dutch snack that costs very little.

What to Skip: Overrated "Free" Attractions

The Bloemenmarkt, or floating flower market, is often listed as a top free attraction. In reality, it has become a crowded tourist trap filled with plastic souvenirs and bulbs that may not clear customs. It is no longer a true floating market as the stalls are permanently fixed on barges. I recommend visiting the Noordermarkt on a Saturday for a more authentic local market experience.

Be cautious of 'free' walking tours that operate in the main squares. While there is no upfront fee, the guides rely entirely on tips and often suggest a minimum of ten to fifteen Euros. This can end up being more expensive than a pre-booked 10 Essential Stops for a 1-Day Amsterdam Walking Tour with a fixed price. If you are on a strict zero-budget plan, it is better to use a free self-guided audio app.

Dam Square is another spot that often feels overwhelming and lacks the charm of the side canals. While it is the historical heart of the city, it is mostly filled with pigeons and expensive chain stores. Walk five minutes in any direction toward the Nine Streets to find much better photo opportunities. Focus your time on the quieter neighborhoods where the real Dutch 'gezelligheid' lives.

Is Amsterdam Expensive for Travelers?

Amsterdam is generally more expensive than Southern European cities like Lisbon or Madrid. However, it remains more affordable than London or Zurich for the savvy budget traveler. A daily budget of 50 to 70 Euros can cover a hostel bed, supermarket food, and transport. Using the free activities on this list will significantly lower your total trip cost.

Accommodation is the biggest expense you will face when visiting the Netherlands. Booking several months in advance is essential to secure the best rates in popular areas. Consider staying in the Amsterdam neighborhoods outside the center to save money. Areas like Amsterdam-West or Noord offer a more local feel at a lower price point.

Museum prices have risen recently, with many major sites now charging over 20 Euros. If you plan to visit many paid sites, the I amsterdam City Card might be worth the investment. For those committed to a free trip, the city's architecture and parks provide plenty of entertainment. You can easily spend four days here without ever paying for an entrance ticket.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the top free attractions in Amsterdam?

The top free attractions include the GVB ferry rides, the Rijksmuseum gardens, and the OBA Central Library. These spots offer iconic views and culture for zero Euros. Most are located near the city center or Central Station.

Are there free walking tours available in Amsterdam?

Yes, several companies offer free walking tours departing from Dam Square. While these tours are technically free to join, guides expect a tip at the end. Budget around 10 to 15 Euros for a good experience.

Can I visit any museums in Amsterdam for free?

Most major museums require a paid ticket for entry. However, you can enjoy the Rijksmuseum gardens and the Civic Guards Gallery for free. These provide a taste of the city's art without the high cost.

Amsterdam is a city that rewards those who look beyond the famous paid attractions. By using the free ferries and exploring the hidden gardens, you can see the true heart of the Dutch capital. I hope this list helps you plan a memorable trip that stays within your budget.

Remember to check the local weather forecast, as many of these activities are best enjoyed on clear days. Whether you are listening to a free concert or wandering the canals, the city's charm is always free to experience. Safe travels and enjoy your time in this beautiful historic city!
