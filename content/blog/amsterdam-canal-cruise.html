What to skip: Common canal cruise mistakes

Skip the Hop-on-Hop-off boat tours if you want a reliable and timely schedule. These boats often get stuck in heavy canal traffic and lack the intimacy of smaller vessels. You are usually better off walking between sights or using the city's efficient tram system.

Avoid booking large glass-topped boats on very rainy days if you want clear photos. The glass often fogs up from the breath of many passengers, obstructing your view of the landmarks. Smaller open boats with umbrellas or partially covered decks often provide a better experience in light rain.

Do not wait until Saturday afternoon to book a themed cruise like a dinner or booze boat. These specialized tours sell out days in advance during the peak summer and festival seasons. A little bit of forward planning ensures you get the exact time slot and boat style you desire.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a typical Amsterdam canal cruise last?

Most standard sightseeing cruises last between 60 and 75 minutes. Specialized dinner or booze cruises often extend to 90 minutes or two hours to allow for a more relaxed pace.

Do I need to book my canal cruise in advance?

While you can buy tickets for standard sightseeing boats at the dock, booking online is highly recommended. Themed cruises and weekend slots often sell out several days in advance during peak season.

Are the canal boats wheelchair accessible?

Many larger operators like Blue Boat and Stromma offer specific boats with wheelchair lifts. It is best to check their websites or call ahead to ensure an accessible vessel is scheduled for your time.
