15 Best Cheap Family Days Out in Nottinghamshire

Nottinghamshire punches well above its weight for affordable family days out. Free country parks, a UNESCO literary trail, volunteer-run mining museums, and a 10-mile waterside cycle route are all on the menu — most for nothing more than a parking fee. This guide focuses on the best low-cost and free experiences across the whole county for 2026, covering everything from the city center to the rural north.

Each entry below has been selected because it costs less than a cinema ticket per person or, in most cases, nothing at all. We've included age-group pointers so you can match the right outing to your kids rather than dragging a toddler up a colliery headstock. Check out our Nottingham family days out guide for complementary city-center ideas and our cheap things to do in Nottingham page for more budget tips.

1. Nottingham City of Literature Map Trail

Nottingham holds UNESCO City of Literature status, and the self-guided map trail is one of the best free activities in the county for school-age children. Download the PDF from the Visit Nottinghamshire official site and follow the route through the Creative Quarter, stopping at literary plaques, sculptures, and independent bookshops. The full circuit takes around two hours at a relaxed pace.

The trail is car-free and walkable entirely from the city tram network, making it one of the cheapest days out available. Children aged 7 and above tend to enjoy spotting the landmarks and reading the quotes aloud. There are several child-friendly cafes along the route if you need a mid-walk break.

Value score: 5/5 — completely free, no booking required. Best for ages 7+.

2. Princess River Cruises on the Trent

Princess River Cruises runs scenic boat trips along the River Trent from the Colwick area of Nottingham. Standard afternoon cruises typically cost £8 to £12 per adult with reduced prices for children, making a family of four viable for around £25 to £30. The 45-minute loop is short enough for younger children and long enough to feel like a proper outing.

Boats run daily throughout summer and on weekends in spring and autumn. Pre-booking online is strongly recommended for Saturday and bank holiday sailings, which fill quickly. The departure point at Colwick has free parking, removing one of the usual budget drains for a family day out.

Value score: 4/5. Best for ages 3 and above. Rainy day note: if the weather turns, the trip is still covered on the lower deck.

3. Thoresby Park and Gallery

Thoresby Park in the north of the county offers free entry to its expansive grounds, a dedicated craft gallery, and the Queen's Royal Lancers Museum. The Robin Hood statue here gives you a much quieter photo opportunity than the famous bronze outside Nottingham Castle, and the grounds are rarely crowded even on summer weekends.

The park is open daily from 09:00 to 17:00. The gallery hours vary by season so check ahead. A small parking charge applies, but the large adventure play area more than justifies the cost for families with children aged 4 to 12.

Value score: 5/5 for the free elements. Best for ages 4 to 12. Located near Ollerton — allow 45 minutes' drive from the city.

4. Go Ape and Sherwood Pines Forest Trails

Sherwood Pines is the county's premier forest park for active families. The walking and cycling trails are completely free once you've paid the parking fee (approximately £3 per hour or £9 for a full day). Go Ape's high ropes course costs extra — expect to pay around £25 to £33 per person depending on the experience selected — but it is not necessary to enjoy a full day here.

The free trails include a dedicated family cycling path, a hidden mushroom sculpture trail, and a Go Wild play area suitable for children aged 3 and above. Bring your own bikes to avoid the rental fees. The park is open from 08:00 until dusk every day except Christmas.

Value score: 4/5 without Go Ape; 3/5 with Go Ape added. Best for ages 3+. Nearest toilets: the main visitor centre, open 09:00 to 16:30.

5. Bestwood Country Park Cycle Route

Bestwood Country Park 20 minutes north of the city centre offers two entirely different cycling experiences in one free site. The "Big Hill" climb up through the former colliery workings rewards older children and adults with panoramic views across the Nottinghamshire landscape. The flat Mill Lakes path around the lower reservoir is ideal for toddlers on balance bikes or families with a pushchair.

Entry is free and the park is open from dawn to sunset daily. The Dynamo House — the last surviving building from Bestwood Colliery — opens for heritage events on occasional weekends with free guided tours. Toilets are located near the Dynamo House and are usually accessible during daylight hours.

Difficulty: Big Hill = moderate (steep sections, suitable ages 8+); Mill Lakes loop = easy (fully flat, pushchair-friendly). Value score: 5/5.

6. Clipstone Headstocks and Raleigh Museum

The twin headstocks at Clipstone are among the largest surviving colliery structures in Europe and make for genuinely impressive industrial heritage viewing. The adjacent Raleigh Museum houses a collection of iconic Nottingham-made Raleigh bicycles alongside local mining artifacts. Both sites are run by volunteers and typically open on specific heritage weekends throughout the year.

Entry is free or by small donation. This site is best suited to children aged 8 and above who can engage with the historical context. Wear closed-toe shoes as the ground around the old colliery structures can be uneven. Check the Prince's Trust Community Heritage page for 2026 opening dates before making the trip.

Value score: 5/5 on open days. Best for ages 8+. Nearest toilets: the heritage centre on site, available during open days only.

7. The Big Track Waterside Cycle Route

The Big Track is a 10-mile circular route following canal towpaths and the banks of the River Trent through the south of the county. The path is entirely free to access and is mostly flat, making it manageable for families cycling with children aged 6 and above. Allow three to four hours for the full loop.

A practical feature that competitors consistently overlook: the route has several marked "escape points" where the path runs close to bus stops on the Nottingham city network. If younger children run out of steam, you can cut the ride short and take the bus back rather than retracing the full distance. Stop at Beeston Lock for a picnic while watching canal boats pass through the lock.

Difficulty: easy to moderate (mostly flat, one gentle climb near Attenborough). Nearest toilets: Beeston Lock and Trent Bridge end of the route. Value score: 5/5.

8. Newark to Harby Cycle Route

This 15-mile traffic-free trail follows a disused railway line east from Newark North Gate station toward the Lincolnshire border. The surface is well maintained and completely flat, making it the best long-distance option for active families with teenagers. The route passes through several small villages where you can find local pubs and shops for mid-ride fuel stops.

Access is free and the path is open 24 hours year-round. Park at Newark North Gate station car park to begin. Families with younger children should treat this as a there-and-back route, turning around at a comfortable halfway point rather than attempting the full 30-mile return in one day.

Difficulty: easy (traffic-free, flat surface). Best for ages 10+ or families with e-bikes. Value score: 5/5 — no cost beyond parking.

9. Cotgrave Canal and Country Park

Cotgrave Country Park occupies the former Cotgrave Colliery site and has been transformed into a wetland habitat with lakes, reed beds, and a network of walking and cycling paths. Entry is free and the park is open daily. The main heron lake is particularly good for birdwatching in spring when grey herons, little egrets, and kingfishers are all active.

The bird hides along the eastern edge of the lake give younger children an enclosed, quiet spot for wildlife photography without disturbing the birds. Most families spend around two hours on the circular trail. A nearby canal path provides an easy link back toward the city for those who want to extend the walk.

Value score: 5/5. Best for nature-focused families with children aged 5+. No toilet facilities on site — use the facilities at nearby Cotgrave Welfare and Sports Ground before entering.

10. Wollaton Hall Walled Garden

The walled kitchen garden at Wollaton Hall is managed by a team of local volunteer gardeners and is separate from the main ticketed house tours. Entry to the garden is free, though a parking charge of £5 applies for the main hall grounds. The garden is open on weekends and select weekdays during the summer growing season — check ahead as hours vary.

Look out for the volunteer-led plant sales held most Saturdays in summer, where you can pick up well-priced herbs and seedlings. Combining the walled garden with the free natural history museum inside Wollaton Hall gives you a full half-day without any ticket cost. The deer that roam the wider park are visible from the garden boundary and are popular with younger visitors.

Value score: 4/5. Best for ages 4+. Guided tours of the hall cost £12 per adult; the garden itself is always free.

11. Nottingham Puppet Festival City Day

The Nottingham Puppet Festival is a biennial event that fills the city centre with giant kinetic sculptures, street theatre, and free outdoor puppet performances. The 2026 festival is scheduled with the main family day centred around the Old Market Square, running from approximately 10:00 to 16:00. Many of the outdoor shows and drop-in workshops are completely free.

The festival also features paid indoor performances at venues including the Lakeside Arts Centre, with tickets typically priced between £6 and £10 per person. Book the indoor shows early as they sell out quickly. The outdoor programme alone justifies the visit and costs nothing beyond transport.

Value score: 5/5 for the free outdoor programme. Best for ages 3 to 10. Check the official Nottingham Puppet Festival website for the confirmed 2026 schedule in spring.

12. Bilsthorpe Heritage Museum

Bilsthorpe Heritage Museum holds a collection of mining memorabilia, photographs, and social history artifacts from the colliery that dominated the village for most of the 20th century. Entry is free, with donations encouraged to help the volunteers maintain the exhibits. The museum is typically open on Sundays and Wednesdays from 10:00 until early afternoon.

What makes this worth the drive from the city is the personal touch: the volunteers are often former miners or their families and share first-hand accounts that no display board can replicate. Children aged 8 and above tend to engage most with the stories. It also serves as an excellent indoor backup if you have planned a Sherwood Pines visit that gets rained off — Bilsthorpe is 15 minutes away by car.

Value score: 5/5. Best for ages 8+.

13. Nottingham Arboretum Tram Trip

Nottingham Arboretum is the city's oldest public park, originally opened in 1852, and features more than 800 individually labelled trees. Entry is free and the park is open daily from 08:00 to dusk. It sits directly on the NET tram line, which means you can combine a tram ride with the visit — popular with younger children who enjoy the journey as much as the destination.

The Victorian bandstand, duck pond, and small playground make this a reliable option for families with children aged 2 to 8. The park is most impressive in spring when the ornamental trees are in blossom and in autumn for the leaf colour. According to the city council's heritage records, the arboretum was one of the inspirations cited in early accounts of Neverland in J.M. Barrie's Peter Pan.

Value score: 5/5. Best for ages 2 to 8. Nearest toilets: the main entrance on Waverley Street, open during park hours.

14. Cheap Places to Eat: Kids Eat Free Deals

Food costs can quietly wreck a family day out budget. Several major retailers and restaurants across Nottinghamshire run "Kids Eat Free" schemes during school holidays and, in some cases, year-round. Morrisons cafes allow one child to eat free per paying adult throughout the school holidays — the Gamston branch near the A52 is convenient after a morning at Attenborough Nature Reserve or Cotgrave Park.

Asda cafes operate a similar scheme during most school holiday periods, with child meals starting from around £1 when accompanied by an adult main. The Victoria shopping centre food court in Nottingham city centre offers several independent traders serving children's meals under £5. Always check the current terms on retailer websites before travelling as the schemes can change seasonally.

Value score: 5/5 when timed correctly. Combine with a free morning at the Arboretum or City of Literature trail for a complete zero-cost city day.

15. Heritage Open Days: The September Window

Heritage Open Days run for ten days every September across England, giving free public access to hundreds of buildings that are usually ticketed or closed to visitors. In Nottinghamshire the annual programme regularly includes The Workhouse at Southwell (normally £10 per adult), Nottingham Castle grounds, private country house gardens, and industrial sites like the Papplewick Pumping Station.

The key tactic is to plan in August. The most popular venues — particularly The Workhouse and anything with a guided underground tour — fill their free timed slots within hours of the programme going live. Register on the national Heritage Open Days website to receive the Nottinghamshire listing the moment it publishes. Families with children aged 8 and above get the most from the guided tours, which are specifically adapted to include younger visitors during the festival window.

Value score: 5/5 for September visitors. Check Nottingham Castle visiting guide for year-round access options outside the September window.

Rainy Day Backup Plan for Every Outdoor Venue

No competitor guide addresses this directly, but every experienced Nottinghamshire parent knows that you need a Plan B when the weather turns. Each of the outdoor entries in this list has a viable indoor fallback within 20 minutes' drive.

  • Sherwood Pines rained off: drive 15 minutes to Bilsthorpe Heritage Museum (free, open Wednesdays and Sundays).
  • Bestwood Country Park too wet: the Dynamo House heritage centre is covered and opens on selected weekends — check ahead.
  • Cotgrave Canal Park flooded: Nottingham Contemporary on the Canal Street waterfront offers free entry and regular free family workshops during school holidays.
  • Thoresby Park rain: the Queen's Royal Lancers Museum inside the estate is fully indoor and free.
  • Big Track too muddy: Nottingham's Central Library on Angel Row has free drop-in activities during school holiday periods and a ground-floor cafe.
  • Wollaton Hall garden closed: the Natural History Museum inside the hall is free and fully covered — the Batman film location alone keeps children occupied for an hour.

The pattern to follow is simple: pair every outdoor booking with one indoor alternative in the same part of the county. Carry the Nottingham Contemporary's events page bookmarked on your phone — they typically post free holiday activities with less than a week's notice.

Practical Tips for Keeping Costs Down in 2026

Transport is often the biggest hidden cost for Nottinghamshire day trips. The Nottingham Express Transit tram network sells a family day ticket covering two adults and up to three children for a fixed price, making the Arboretum, city centre trails, and Nottingham Contemporary all accessible without a car. For routes north toward Bestwood and Sherwood Pines, the Nottingham City Transport bus network covers most major sites with a Rider ticket valid across all routes. The county council's holiday guide also lists seasonal transport discounts.

Parking at National Trust sites — Clumber Park and Sherwood Forest both apply — is free for members. An NT family membership costs around £130 per year; if you visit four or five NT sites it pays for itself. Non-members can avoid paying at Sherwood Forest by cycling in from Edwinstowe village, which is free to enter from the street.

Pack a proper cooler bag. Snacks and drinks at forest visitor centres and country park cafes carry a significant premium. A family of four spending £8 at a supermarket the evening before can avoid £25 to £35 in on-site food costs. Use that saving for one ticketed experience like Go Ape or a Princess River Cruise. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best free thing to do in Nottinghamshire?

Sherwood Forest is the top free attraction, offering miles of trails and the famous Major Oak. While parking has a fee, entry to the forest and the visitor center is completely free for all families. It is a perfect spot for a budget-friendly picnic.

Are there any cheap indoor activities for rainy days?

The Nottingham Contemporary and the Natural History Museum at Wollaton Hall both offer free entry for families. These venues provide excellent shelter and educational exhibits when the weather turns. Always check for free seasonal workshops during school holidays.

How can I save money on transport in Nottingham?

Purchase a family day ticket for the Nottingham Express Transit tram system to save on city travel. This ticket allows up to two adults and three children to travel all day for a fixed low price. It is much cheaper than paying for central car parking.

From the historic trails of the city center to the deep woods of Sherwood, the variety is impressive. By focusing on free parks and using the tram, you can keep your daily costs surprisingly low.
