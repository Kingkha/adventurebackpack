10 Essential Nottingham Caves Tour Tips and Attractions

Nottingham sits on a soft sandstone foundation filled with over 800 hidden caverns. Exploring these underground spaces on a nottingham caves tour reveals centuries of local history.

You will find everything from medieval workshops to modern bomb shelters beneath the city streets. These sites offer a unique perspective on how residents lived and worked throughout the ages.

Visitors often find the subterranean climate refreshing during the warmer summer months. This guide covers the essential stops, practical ticket prices, and navigational details for anyone curious about the city's hidden depths.

Overview of Nottingham Caves Tour Options

Several distinct experiences allow you to venture beneath the pavement in this historic city. The most popular choice is the City of Caves, located near the Lace Market district. Other options include guided walks through the castle's defensive tunnels and haunted cellar tours.

Each location provides a different narrative, from industrial heritage to legendary myths. You can choose between self-guided audio tours or expert-led group walks depending on the site. Most tours require pre-booking to ensure a spot during busy weekend periods.

There is no single ticket covering all sites. Plan your day around one main cave experience and build outward from there. The City of Caves and Nottingham Castle tours are each roughly 45–60 minutes, so combining both in a single afternoon is comfortably achievable.

City of Caves: Exploring the Subterranean Network

This attraction features the largest public section of the city's vast sandstone tunnel system. Walking through the City of Caves allows you to see a rare medieval tannery, WWII air-raid shelters, and Anglo-Saxon dwelling spaces carved directly into the rock. The audio guide narrates the stories of the people who once lived and worked in these hollowed-out chambers.

Bring your own headphones. The cave interiors are naturally echoey, and the built-in speakers on shared devices struggle to cut through the reverb. Personal earphones make a significant difference to how clearly you follow the narration.

Tours depart roughly every 20 minutes throughout the day. Groups are capped at around 20 people, so popular slots sell out quickly on weekends and during school holidays. The site operates daily 10:00–17:00, with last entry at 16:10, 365 days a year except 24–26 December and 1 January.

Wear sturdy shoes. The path is uneven sandstone throughout, and some passages are low enough to require ducking. Expect to spend about 45 minutes on the main route, with a short free-roam section at the end.

Practical Planning: Tickets, Prices, and New Entrance Location

Navigation can be tricky because the old entrance through the Broadmarsh Shopping Centre is now permanently closed. Find the new entrance at the bottom of Garner's Hill Steps, postcode NG1 1HF. Google Maps frequently still directs visitors toward the demolished Broadmarsh site — ignore it. When you reach Weekday Cross, walk toward Nottingham Contemporary gallery and head down the steps to your left.

The City of Caves is entirely cashless. If you only have cash, walk five minutes to the National Justice Museum desk and pay there. There are no toilets on site either; use the facilities at the National Justice Museum before you descend.

Booking your nottingham tunnels underground tour online in advance is the safest approach. Current 2026 pricing at the City of Caves: adults £11, children aged 5–17 £9, concessions £10. Family tickets run from £25 (one adult, three children) to £35.50 (two adults, three children). A joint ticket covering the City of Caves and the National Justice Museum costs £21 for adults and £17 for children. All standard admission tickets include unlimited re-entry for one full year from the date of purchase — a detail worth noting if you live nearby or plan to return.

Check the nationaljusticemuseum.org.uk site for the most current rates and to book timed slots in advance.

National Justice Museum: History and Cave Access

The National Justice Museum serves as the primary logistics hub for cave visitors. It is located in a former Victorian courtroom and manages the nearby City of Caves site. Buy joint tickets here, pay with cash, or use the toilet facilities — none of which are available underground.

Exploring the museum's galleries offers a grim look at crime and punishment through the centuries. Costumed actors bring the dark history of the gaol to life in the restored courtroom and prison cells. It is a natural companion to the subterranean experience and extends the historical narrative well beyond what the caves alone provide.

You can find current pricing and schedule details on the nationaljusticemuseum.org.uk official portal. Staff members provide maps and directions to help you navigate the winding streets of the Lace Market to the cave entrance.

Nottingham Castle: Mortimer's Hole and Medieval Foundations

The castle grounds hide a complex network of tunnels used for defense and secret escapes. A dedicated nottingham castle cave tour takes you deep into the rock beneath the mansion. You will learn about the dramatic capture of Roger Mortimer in 1330, when a party of soldiers entered through these tunnels at night to arrest Queen Isabella's lover.

This specific route involves climbing many steep steps, so a moderate level of fitness is required. Guides share details about the medieval foundations and the castle's strategic importance as a fortified position overlooking the city. The tour is suitable for families and young children, though the steps are non-negotiable.

You can find current pricing and schedule details on the nottinghamcastle.org.uk official portal. Tickets for these specialized tours are sold separately from general castle admission and often sell out ahead of busy weekends. Plan for roughly an hour to complete the guided underground journey, and allow time to enjoy the views from the castle terrace above afterward.

The Original Nottingham Ghost Walk: Haunted Cave Stories

For those seeking a thrill, the evening ghost walks explore the city's most haunted cellars and courtyards. The walk runs every Saturday and starts at Ye Olde Trip to Jerusalem, a pub built directly into the cliffside of Castle Rock. It is famous for the haunted caves beneath the building, and the guide starts with those stories before moving into the surrounding Castle Quarter streets.

The route is easy walking, covers about half a mile, and takes 90 minutes to two hours. It is one of the most popular nottingham things to do at night for visitors who want atmosphere alongside history. Hearing spooky legends while standing in a cold sandstone chamber, with the city noise muffled above you, adds something no daytime tour can replicate.

Professional guides lead you through narrow alleyways and hidden courtyards as the sun sets. Wear a warm jacket regardless of season — the temperature underground stays low year-round — and book ahead as Saturday slots fill quickly during summer.

What First-Timers Often Miss About the Caves

The 20-person cap per tour group is not just a comfort measure — it also means the guide can take questions throughout rather than rushing everyone forward. Smaller groups routinely get longer explanations and are often shown side chambers that larger groups skip. If you are booking for a party of more than eight, consider splitting into adjacent time slots to get this benefit.

Many visitors do not realise that privately owned caves exist throughout the city. Under English property law, if you purchase a house that sits above a cave system, the cave belongs to you. Nottingham has hundreds of such privately-held caverns beneath ordinary terraced streets that will never appear on any tour itinerary. The guides at the City of Caves often reference this during the section on cave ownership history, and it reframes the entire underground landscape in an unexpected way.

One more overlooked detail: the annual unlimited re-entry included with every standard ticket. If you visit once and want to bring a friend back later in the year, your original ticket still works. No other major cave site in England currently offers this policy, and it is genuinely useful for Nottingham residents or repeat visitors to the East Midlands.

Lace Market Heritage: St Mary's Church and Street Murals

After your underground adventure, take time to explore the surrounding Lace Market district. This area was once the world center of the lace industry during the Victorian era. You can find many nottingham free things to do by simply walking the historic streets here.

St Mary's Church is the oldest religious foundation in the city and features stunning gothic architecture. Its large windows and peaceful atmosphere provide a sharp contrast to the cramped cave passages below. The church is open to the public during daylight hours.

Keep an eye out for vibrant street murals that decorate the brick walls of former lace warehouses. These modern artworks celebrate the city's creative spirit and make for excellent photography. The mix of industrial Victorian buildings and contemporary art makes this neighborhood worth lingering in before or after your tour.

Unique Local Stops: Debbie Bryan and Watson Fothergill

Visit the Debbie Bryan shop for a relaxing afternoon tea and traditional craft session. This award-winning spot offers heritage craft kits — many of which draw directly on the lace-making traditions of the district. It is an ideal place to rest your feet after the underground tunnels and connects naturally to the historical narrative you have just walked through.

Architecture fans should look for the distinctive buildings designed by Watson Fothergill. His former head office, a short walk from the cave entrance, features elaborate gothic revival details and whimsical stone carvings you will not find anywhere else in the city center. You can spot his unique style on several other structures nearby, and identifying them becomes a satisfying side-game during any walking tour.

Industrial and Literary History: Clipstone and D.H. Lawrence

Travelers with a car might enjoy a trip to see the Clipstone Headstocks nearby. These towering steel winding towers represent the region's coal mining heritage and offer a stark visual contrast to the ancient hand-carved sandstone caves in the city. The difference between the two kinds of extraction — medieval sandstone carved by hand versus industrial coal hauled by steam — sharpens your appreciation of both sites.

Literary enthusiasts should visit the D.H. Lawrence Birthplace Museum in the nearby town of Eastwood. This site explores the humble beginnings of one of England's most famous writers and the working-class life that shaped his novels. Both locations require a short drive but are worth the detour if you have a full day in the area.

Modern Nottingham: Brian Clough Statue and Escapologic

Head toward Old Market Square to see the famous statue of football manager Brian Clough. This landmark is a popular meeting point for locals and sits near the Council House, the grand centerpiece of the city's main plaza. It is a natural finishing point for a long day of underground and street-level exploration.

If you want more underground excitement, try one of the immersive rooms at Escapologic. This is one of the top nottingham things to do adults will enjoy for a high-energy challenge. These fictional escape games use detailed underground-themed sets and provide a playful counterpoint to the real history you have spent the day absorbing.

The city center also offers a wide variety of modern shops and diverse dining options within a short walk. This blend of ancient history and modern energy makes Nottingham a compelling destination for a full weekend rather than a single afternoon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the entrance to the City of Caves?

The entrance is located at the bottom of Garner's Hill Steps, near the Nottingham Contemporary art gallery (postcode NG1 1HF). Do not follow older maps or Google Maps directing you to the Broadmarsh Shopping Centre as that entrance is permanently closed. You can find more details in our City of Caves guide for planning.

Are the Nottingham cave tours accessible for everyone?

Most cave sites involve uneven ground and steep steps, which may be difficult for those with mobility issues. The City of Caves has no wheelchair or pushchair access — the entrance itself involves multiple steps and the interior paths are uneven sandstone. Always check the official site for specific accessibility updates before you book your visit.

How long does a typical Nottingham caves tour last?

A standard self-guided tour through the City of Caves usually takes about 45 minutes, with tours departing every 20 minutes. Guided tours at Nottingham Castle last around 25 minutes underground, and evening ghost walks run 90 minutes to two hours. Allow extra time for walking between the different historical sites in the city center.

Can I buy tickets for the caves on the day of my visit?

Walk-up tickets are sometimes available but booking online in advance is strongly recommended. During busy weekends or school holidays, popular time slots sell out. The site is cashless — if you only have cash, pay at the National Justice Museum desk, around five minutes' walk from the cave entrance.

How much do City of Caves tickets cost in 2026?

In 2026, adult tickets are £11, children aged 5–17 are £9, and concessions are £10. Family tickets range from £25 (one adult, three children) to £35.50 (two adults, three children). A joint ticket with the National Justice Museum costs £21 for adults and £17 for children. All tickets include unlimited re-entry to the City of Caves for one year from the date of purchase.

Are there toilets at the City of Caves?

There are no toilets on site at the City of Caves. Use the facilities at the National Justice Museum, which is approximately five minutes' walk away. Plan this stop before you descend, as there is no option underground.

Exploring the underground world of Nottingham offers a fascinating look at the city's hidden heritage. From medieval tanneries to secret castle tunnels, there is something for every history lover to enjoy.

Remember to navigate toward Garner's Hill Steps (NG1 1HF) rather than Broadmarsh, bring your own headphones for the audio guide, and use the National Justice Museum for any cash payment or toilet needs before you go underground. These practical details make the difference between a smooth visit and a frustrating start.

Whether you are visiting for a day or a weekend, the caves remain a must-see highlight of the region. Book your timed slot in advance and arrive knowing the 2026 prices and layout — your subterranean adventure will be all the better for it.
